A work in progress

For the families who carry ALS care home.

A diagnosis ends the conversation at the hospital. For the family, it starts years of care at home, often learned alone and in a hurry. ALSconnect is being built so they don't have to work it all out from scratch.

2:07 am

A machine alarm goes off in the next room. You don't know if it's serious. You don't know who to call. Everyone you could ask is asleep.

This is the moment ALSconnect is being designed for.

The problem

What nobody explains after the diagnosis

ALS has no cure, and it changes month by month. The care it needs moves into the home, and most of it falls on a family member with no training and no plan.

There is no map

Families leave the clinic with a name for the disease and little else. What home care will look like, what equipment they'll need, and who can help nearby are left for them to discover.

The answers are scattered

What families know lives in group chats and in the memories of people who have been through it. The same questions get asked again and again, often late at night.

Caregivers carry it alone

The person doing the caring loses sleep, work and support along the way. Very little around a serious illness is built with them in mind.

The vision

What we want to be true

No family in India should have to figure out ALS care from scratch, on their own, in the middle of the night.

  • The right help at the right moment. Support that meets a family where they are in the journey, at the time they need it.
  • Local and trustworthy. Help that is real in your own city, checked by people who know, and kept up to date.
  • Built with caregivers. Shaped by the families who have lived it, so what one family learns can help the next.

How it reaches people

Going where families already are

Caregivers don't have time to learn a new tool in a crisis, so ALSconnect will meet them in the places they already turn to.

Caregiver communities

Working alongside the support groups and NGOs families already trust, instead of competing with them.

Doctors and clinics

Reaching families at the moment of diagnosis, when they first need to know what comes next.

On the phone in their hand

Simple, light and easy to use on any phone, at any hour, without a long sign-up.

Where it is today

Early, careful, and still listening

ALSconnect is a student-led project that started from one family's experience of caring for someone with ALS. It is still being shaped, and nothing is live yet.

The steps don't run in a straight line. Listening and designing happen side by side, and what caregivers say keeps changing what gets built.

  1. 1

    Listening to caregivers Ongoing

    Still talking with families and learning from caregiver communities. Each conversation feeds back into the design.

  2. 2

    Mapping the journey

    Following the path of care from the first symptoms to life after loss, and where families get left behind.

  3. 3

    Designing the service Now

    Working out what help should look like and how it stays accurate.

  4. 4

    Testing with families

    Trying early versions with caregivers and the clinicians who support them.

  5. 5

    A small pilot

    Starting in a few cities, then growing carefully.
